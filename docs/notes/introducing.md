# Introducing Yourself in Chinese

## Summary

* **你好** is the safest neutral greeting; **您好** is polite but can sound formal.
* **我叫……** introduces your given full name naturally; **我是……** often introduces identity, role, or nationality.
* **你叫什么名字？** is clear but direct; **您贵姓？** asks politely for someone’s surname.
* For origin, **我是……人** is the most natural basic pattern.
* **再见** is standard; **拜拜** is casual and common.


## Greetings

| Function          | Chinese | Pinyin                 | Meaning                 | Register        |
| ----------------- | ------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------- |
| Greeting          | 你好      | nǐ hǎo                 | hello                   | neutral         |
| Greeting (polite) | 您好      | nín hǎo                | hello                   | polite/formal   |
| Greeting          | 大家好     | dàjiā hǎo             | hello                   | polite/formal   |

**你好** is the default greeting for one person. It is safe in most situations, though in close relationships people often skip formal greetings and go straight into the conversation.

**您好** uses the polite pronoun **您**. It is appropriate with customers, elders, teachers, officials, or people you want to show respect to. Among peers, it may sound distant or overly formal.

**大家好** is used when greeting a group, especially before introducing yourself in class, at a meeting, or in a video.


## My name is... / What is your name?

| Function        | Chinese | Pinyin                 | Meaning                 | Register        |
| ----------------- | ------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------- |
| Name            | 我叫……    | wǒ jiào ...            | my name is ...          | neutral         |
| Ask name        | 你叫什么名字？ | nǐ jiào shénme míngzi? | what’s your name?       | direct, common  |
| Surname         | 我姓……    | wǒ xìng ...            | my name is ...          | neutral         |
| Ask surname     | 您贵姓？    | nín guìxìng?           | may I ask your surname? | polite/formal   |

**我叫……** is the most basic and natural way to state your name.

**我姓……** gives only your surname. This is useful when names are handled formally or when the surname matters.

**我是……** can introduce your name, but it often sounds like “I am ...” in the sense of identity, role, or speaker identification. It is natural in contexts like phone calls, meetings, and introductions: **我是王明。**

To ask someone’s name, **你叫什么名字？** is common and clear. With adults in formal settings, it can sound too direct. **您贵姓？** is more polite, but it asks only for the surname.

!!! example
你好，我叫王明。
Nǐ hǎo, wǒ jiào Wáng Míng.
Hello, my name is Wang Ming.

!!! example
您好，我叫李华。
Nín hǎo, wǒ jiào Lǐ Huá.
Hello, my name is Li Hua.

!!! example
大家好，我叫安娜。
Dàjiā hǎo, wǒ jiào Ānnà.
Hello everyone, my name is Anna.

!!! example
我叫王明。
Wǒ jiào Wáng Míng.
My name is Wang Ming.

!!! example
你好，你叫什么名字？
Nǐ hǎo, nǐ jiào shénme míngzi?
Hello, what’s your name?

!!! example
我姓李。
Wǒ xìng Lǐ.
My surname is Li.

!!! example
您好，您贵姓？
Nín hǎo, nín guìxìng?
Hello, may I ask your surname?

!!! example
你叫王明吗？
Nǐ jiào Wáng Míng ma?
Is your name Wang Ming?

!!! example
你叫安娜吗？
Nǐ jiào Ānnà ma?
Is your name Anna?

!!! example
您叫李华吗？
Nín jiào Lǐ Huá ma?
Is your name Li Hua?

!!! example
你是王明吗？
Nǐ shì Wáng Míng ma?
Are you Wang Ming?


## Where you are from

| Function        | Chinese | Pinyin                 | Meaning                 | Register        |
| ----------------- | ------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------- |
| Origin          | 我是……人   | wǒ shì ... rén         | I’m from ...            | natural         |
| Origin          | 我来自……   | wǒ láizì ...           | I come from ...         | slightly formal |

**我是……人** is the most natural basic pattern.

**我来自……** means “I come from ...” and is slightly more formal or presentation-like. It is common in self-introductions, speeches, and written profiles.

!!! example
我是德国人。
Wǒ shì Déguó rén.
I’m German.

!!! example
我是柏林人。
Wǒ shì Bólín rén.
I’m from Berlin.

!!! example
我是美国人。
Wǒ shì Měiguó rén.
I’m American.

!!! example
我是德国人。
Wǒ shì Déguó rén.
I’m German.

!!! example
你是美国人吗？
Nǐ shì Měiguó rén ma?
Are you American?

!!! example
我来自柏林。
Wǒ láizì Bólín.
I come from Berlin.

!!! example
你来自北京吗？
Nǐ láizì Běijīng ma?
Do you come from Beijing?




### 4. Goodbye

| Function        | Chinese | Pinyin                 | Meaning                 | Register        |
| --------------- | ------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------- |
| Goodbye         | 再见      | zàijiàn                | goodbye                 | neutral         |
| Casual goodbye  | 拜拜      | báibái                 | bye-bye                 | casual          |
| See you later   | 回头见     | huítóu jiàn            | see you later           | colloquial      |
| Leaving first   | 先走了     | xiān zǒu le            | I’m leaving first       | colloquial      |

**再见** is standard and neutral.

**拜拜** is casual and very common, especially among friends, classmates, and younger speakers.

**回头见** means “see you later” or “see you next time.” It is colloquial and assumes future contact.

**先走了** means “I’m leaving first.” It is natural when you leave before the other person or group.


## Core pattern and examples

**你好，我叫……。我是……人。很高兴认识你。再见。**
Nǐ hǎo, wǒ jiào ... Wǒ shì ... rén. Hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ. Zàijiàn.
Hello, my name is ... I’m from ... Nice to meet you. Goodbye.

!!! example "Example 1"
你好，我叫李明。
Nǐ hǎo, wǒ jiào Lǐ Míng.
Hello, my name is Li Ming.

```
**我叫……** is the safest basic pattern for giving your name.
```

!!! example "Example 2"
您好，我姓王。
Nín hǎo, wǒ xìng Wáng.
Hello, my surname is Wang.

```
**您好** and **我姓……** fit a more polite or formal setting.
```

!!! example "Example 3"
你叫什么名字？
Nǐ jiào shénme míngzi?
What’s your name?

```
Natural and common, but direct. With strangers in formal settings, use a softer form.
```

!!! example "Example 4"
请问，您贵姓？
Qǐngwèn, nín guìxìng?
May I ask your surname?

```
**请问** softens the question. **贵姓** is polite and formal.
```

!!! example "Example 5"
大家好，我叫陈雨，是上海人。
Dàjiā hǎo, wǒ jiào Chén Yǔ, shì Shànghǎi rén.
Hello everyone, my name is Chen Yu, and I’m from Shanghai.

```
**大家好** is natural when speaking to a group.
```

!!! example "Example 6"
我是德国人，现在住在北京。
Wǒ shì Déguó rén, xiànzài zhù zài Běijīng.
I’m German, and I live in Beijing now.

```
**我是……人** gives nationality or place of origin. **住在……** gives current residence.
```

!!! example "Example 7"
我来自柏林。
Wǒ láizì Bólín.
I come from Berlin.

```
**来自** is clear but slightly more formal than **我是柏林人**.
```

!!! example "Example 8"
很高兴认识你。
Hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ.
Nice to meet you.

```
Standard after exchanging names. **认识** here means “to meet / get to know.”
```

!!! example "Example 9"
我先走了，再见。
Wǒ xiān zǒu le, zàijiàn.
I’m leaving now. Goodbye.

```
**先走了** is natural when leaving before the other person.
```

!!! example "Example 10"
好，回头见。
Hǎo, huítóu jiàn.
Okay, see you later.

```
**回头见** is casual and implies you expect to meet or talk again.
```

## Common errors / pitfalls

!!! warning "Do not overuse 你好吗？"
**你好吗？** means “How are you?”, but it is not the default casual greeting in many everyday Chinese interactions. It can sound like a translation of English. **你好** is safer for a basic greeting.

!!! warning "我叫 is not the same as 我是"
**我叫李明。**
Wǒ jiào Lǐ Míng.
My name is Li Ming.

```
**我是李明。**
Wǒ shì Lǐ Míng.
I’m Li Ming.

**我是李明** is natural when identifying yourself, for example on the phone, at a meeting, or when someone expects that identity.
```

!!! warning "您贵姓 asks for surname, not full name"
**您贵姓？**
Nín guìxìng?
May I ask your surname?

```
A natural answer is **我姓王**, not necessarily the full name.
```

!!! warning "我从德国来 may describe travel route"
**我是德国人** = I am German.
**我来自德国** = I come from Germany.
**我从德国来** = I came from Germany.

```
The last sentence can refer to the place you just traveled from.
```

## Comparison

### 你好 vs 您好 vs 大家好

| Form | Use        | Nuance                                    |
| ---- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| 你好   | one person | neutral, safest default                   |
| 您好   | one person | polite, respectful, sometimes formal      |
| 大家好  | group      | natural for public or group introductions |

### 我叫…… vs 我姓…… vs 我是……

| Form  | Meaning            | Best used when                         |
| ----- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| 我叫李明  | My name is Li Ming | giving your full name                  |
| 我姓李   | My surname is Li   | surname is enough or formally relevant |
| 我是李明  | I am Li Ming       | identifying yourself in context        |
| 我是学生  | I am a student     | giving role or identity                |
| 我是德国人 | I am German        | giving nationality or origin           |

| ------- | ------------------------- | ------------------- |

| 怎么称呼您？  | How should I address you? | polite, flexible    |

## Notes

!!! tip "A compact natural self-introduction"
你好，我叫安娜，是德国人，现在住在北京。很高兴认识你。
Nǐ hǎo, wǒ jiào Ānnà, shì Déguó rén, xiànzài zhù zài Běijīng. Hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ.
Hello, my name is Anna. I’m German and live in Beijing now. Nice to meet you.

!!! note "Name order"
Chinese names normally place the surname first: **王明** = Wang Ming, where **王** is the surname. Foreign names are usually transliterated and may follow the original order in casual use.

!!! note "Tone and rhythm"
In **你好**, both syllables are third tone in isolation, but in actual speech **nǐ** is usually pronounced with a rising tone before another third tone: **ní hǎo** in surface pronunciation. The standard written pinyin remains **nǐ hǎo**.
