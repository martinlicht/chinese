

# Comparing 二 and 两

Mandarin has two common words for “two”: **二** and **两**. They are not interchangeable in all situations.

**二** is the basic numeral. It is used when reading numbers, counting in sequence, giving phone numbers, saying ordinal numbers, and reading digits.

**两** is common when saying “two of something,” especially before measure words and many nouns.

| Chinese | Pinyin | Meaning |
| ------- | ------ | ------- |
| 二       | èr     | two     |
| 两       | liǎng  | two     |

!!! tip
    As a first rule, use **二** for the digit “2,” but use **两** before measure words when counting things.

## Uses of 二

**二** is used for the digit **2**. It appears in counting sequences, phone numbers, dates, ordinal expressions, room numbers, floor numbers, and larger numbers such as **十二** and **二十**.

!!! example "Counting in sequence"
    {{zh:一、二、三。}}  
    Yī, èr, sān.  
    One, two, three.  

    In a counting sequence, use **二**, not **两**.

!!! example "Ordinal numbers"
    {{zh:第二}}  
    dì-èr  
    second; number two  

    With **第**, use **二** to form ordinal expressions.

!!! example "The second one"
    {{zh:第二个}}  
    dì-èr gè  
    the second one  

    This means “the second one,” not “two items.”

!!! example "The second time"
    {{zh:第二次}}  
    dì-èr cì  
    the second time  

    Ordinal expressions use **二**.

!!! example "February"
    {{zh:二月}}  
    Èryuè  
    February  

    February is literally “the second month.”

!!! example "The second day of the month"
    {{zh:二号}}  
    èr hào  
    the second day of the month; number 2  

    Dates and numbers commonly use **二**.

!!! example "Metro line 2"
    {{zh:二号线}}  
    èr hào xiàn  
    Line 2  

    Numbered lines use **二**.

!!! example "Second floor"
    {{zh:二楼}}  
    èr lóu  
    second floor  

    Floor numbers commonly use **二**.
    
!!! example "Second page"
    {{zh:第二百页}}  
    dì-èr bǎi yè  
    page 200

## 二 in larger numbers

Larger numbers that contain the digit **2** also use **二**.

!!! example "Twelve"
    {{zh:十二}}  
    shí'èr  
    twelve  

    The final digit “2” is read **二**.

!!! example "Twenty"
    {{zh:二十}}  
    èrshí  
    twenty  

    In **二十**, use **二**.

!!! example "Twelve yuan"
    {{zh:十二块钱}}  
    shí'èr kuài qián  
    twelve yuan  

    Even before a measure word, **十二** stays **十二**.

!!! example "Twenty yuan"
    {{zh:二十块钱}}  
    èrshí kuài qián  
    twenty yuan  

    **二十** is followed by the measure word **块**, but **二** is still used inside the number.

!!! example "Twenty-two people"
    {{zh:二十二个人}}  
    èrshí-èr gè rén  
    twenty-two people  

    Numbers ending in **二**, such as **二十二**, can be followed by measure words.

## Uses of 两

When saying “two of something,” Mandarin usually uses **两** before a measure word.

!!! example "Two hours"
    {{zh:两个小时}}  
    liǎng gè xiǎoshí  
    two hours  

    Before the measure word **个**, use **两**.

!!! example "Two o'clock"
    {{zh:两点}}  
    liǎng diǎn  
    two o'clock  

    For time of day, use **两点**, not usually **二点**.

!!! example "Two days"
    {{zh:两天}}  
    liǎng tiān  
    two days  

    With time duration, **两** is common.

!!! example "Two weeks"
    {{zh:两个星期}}  
    liǎng gè xīngqī  
    two weeks  

    Use **两** before **个星期**.

!!! example "Two months"
    {{zh:两个月}}  
    liǎng gè yuè  
    two months  

    Use **两** before **个月**.

!!! example "Two years"
    {{zh:两年}}  
    liǎng nián  
    two years  

    With **年**, use **两** for “two years.”

!!! example "Twice"
    {{zh:两次}}  
    liǎng cì  
    two times; twice  

    Use **两** before the measure word **次**.

!!! example "Two yuan"
    {{zh:两块钱}}  
    liǎng kuài qián  
    two yuan  

    For “two yuan,” ordinary Mandarin uses **两块钱**.

!!! example "Two hundred"
    {{zh:两百}}  
    liǎng bǎi  
    two hundred  

    **二百** is also possible, but **两百** is very common.

!!! example "Two thousand"
    {{zh:两千}}  
    liǎng qiān  
    two thousand  

    {{zh:两万个人}}  
    liǎng wàn gè rén  
    twenty thousand people  
    
!!! example "Two million"
    {{zh:两亿人口}}  
    liǎng yì rénkǒu  
    two hundred million people

    Before **千**, **两** is common.



!!! example "Both of them"
    {{zh:我两个都要。}}  
    Wǒ liǎng gè dōu yào.  
    I want both of them.  

    **两个都** means “both.”

## Main contrast

| Use | Usually use | Example | Meaning |
| --- | ----------- | ------- | ------- |
| Counting sequence | 二 | {{zh:一、二、三}} | one, two, three |
| Digit reading | 二 | {{zh:二零二}} | 202 |
| Ordinal numbers | 二 | {{zh:第二}} | second |
| Larger numbers | 二 | {{zh:十二}} | twelve |
| Quantity before measure words | 两 | {{zh:两个人}} | two people |
| Time of day | 两 | {{zh:两点}} | two o'clock |
| “Both” | 两 | {{zh:两个都}} | both |

!!! tip
    **第二个** means “the second one.”  
    **两个** means “two of them.”
